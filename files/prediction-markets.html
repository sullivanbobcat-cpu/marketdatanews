SEC/CFTC Joint Project Crypto Summit
CFTC Chair Selig announces four-part regulatory agenda for prediction markets at joint summit with SEC. Framework to address manipulation, insider trading, government official trading restrictions, and contract self-certification standards.
February 25, 2026
CFTC Enforcement Advisory Issued
CFTC issues formal enforcement advisory clarifying full enforcement authority over CFTC-registered designated contract markets. Kalshi reports two insider trading cases referred to enforcement. CFTC confirms ability to bring manipulation charges against prediction market participants.

CFTC issues ANPRM on event contract regulation. Guidance Advisory 26-08 simultaneously issued to all DCMs establishing standards for manipulation resistance in binary contract markets. Comment period opened for sports contracts, insider trading rules, and restrictions on trading by government officials.

5c(c) Capital Fund Launched
New venture fund backed by Kalshi CEO Tarek Mansour, Polymarket CEO Shayne Coplan, Marc Andreessen, and Ribbit Capital. Named after CFTC section governing event contracts. Fund targets prediction market infrastructure and adjacent fintech investments.

Coinbase Nevada Preliminary Injunction
Nevada court issues preliminary injunction against Coinbase prediction market product. Coinbase immediately files preemptive federal lawsuit asserting CFTC registration preempts state gaming law. Case expected to be a bellwether for federal vs. state jurisdiction.
March 27, 2026
Washington AG Files Against Kalshi
Washington State Attorney General files action against Kalshi over sports event contracts. Sixth state to take formal action. Kalshi argues federal preemption under CFTC registration. Case joins existing proceedings in New York, Massachusetts, Arizona, and Nevada.

CFTC accepting public comment on: sports event contract standards; insider trading rules for event contracts; restrictions on trading by government officials and their immediate families; and manipulation resistance standards for DCM rule self-certification.

Market Data Infrastructure Implications

Feed Infrastructure
Exchange-Grade Data Requirements Emerging
Prediction markets generate real-time binary contract price data with increasing velocity. As platforms scale to billions in daily volume — Polymarket hit $425M in a single day on February 28, 2026 — feed infrastructure requirements are approaching those of traditional exchanges. Firms building on these platforms need low-latency data feeds, historical data archives, normalized order book data, and surveillance capabilities equivalent to regulated equities venues.
Market Data Costs
Free API Access Won't Last
Unlike traditional exchanges, Kalshi and Polymarket currently offer free REST and WebSocket API access to real-time and historical data. This pricing model will not persist as platforms mature, seek revenue diversification, and face the infrastructure costs of serving institutional clients. Market data professionals should document current data consumption patterns now — before fee schedules are introduced. Early API integrations may be grandfathered; late adopters will pay institutional rates.
Compliance Implications
DCM Obligations Apply in Full
CFTC-registered DCMs are required to maintain complete audit trails, conduct ongoing market surveillance, and enforce rules against manipulation and insider trading. Firms trading on prediction markets face the same reporting obligations as traditional derivatives markets, including position reporting and large trader notification thresholds. Under proposed CFTC rules, CAT reporting requirements may extend to event contracts. Compliance teams should treat prediction market activity as equivalent to futures trading for internal policy purposes.
